falsch finds the word in every spelling the texts use: Urtheil and Urteil, Wiederspruch and Widerspruch, Principium and Prinzip, Saz and Satz, are one word to the search. fals* finds every word that begins so. Several words, form* fals*, find places where they stand near each other, in any order. Quotation marks, "der form nach", keep words together and in order. OR joins alternatives. A regular expression between slashes, /formali?ter/, is matched against the text as printed.

With English on in the header, a choice beside the box, German | English, says which the question is put to. German, the default, is the search described above. English searches the English translation of every passage that has one, so an English word, judgment, "principle of contradiction", certain*, finds the German passages it translates. An English word finds its plain plural too. Such a hit is marked found in the English, with its English and roughly the stretch of German it translates. The English is Jesus’s machine translation, and Baumgarten and Kant’s notes on Baumgarten have none yet.

A page of the Academy edition, written as scholars cite it, goes straight to that page: 24:892, 24.892, AA 24:892, Ak. 16, 236 or AA XVII 45 opens the text printed there, where the page begins. A page of Kant’s notes opens the textbook they are written in, with the note beside it. A Reflexion’s number, Refl. 4086, R 4086 or 4086 alone, opens that note on the right.

Overview shows every distinct phrasing the search found and where it occurs; Passages lists them. Click a passage to read it in its full text on the right; Connections there shows the textbook § it belongs to, Kant’s notes on it, the other texts on the same §, and its parallel passages. Select words in any text to search for them.

Compare, beside Full text, keeps the passage in view and lists under it what shares its wording: its textbook §, Kant’s notes (filed under the same § or not), the lectures and Jäsche. Pick one, or name one (Refl. 4086, 16:236, Meier § 125), to see it beneath with the words the two share marked in both. Click a sentence to compare it alone. C opens Compare and goes back; the arrows keep it open from result to result.
