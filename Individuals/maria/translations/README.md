# English translations (not displayed)

Machine translations of three texts, saved as static data. The three `*-en.json` files are
for later use only: **nothing loads them, and nothing from them is displayed on the site.**
The one exception is `meier-aligned.js` (below), which `busolt-reader.html` loads so that a
Meier sentence, hovered or clicked, can float its English counterpart over it.

| File | Text | Source ids |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `busolt-en.json` | Logik Busolt (AA 24:603–686) | block ids of `data/logik-busolt-24.js` |
| `blomberg-en.json` | Logik Blomberg (AA 24:7–301) | block ids of `data/logik-blomberg-24.js` |
| `meier-en.json` | Meier, *Auszug aus der Vernunftlehre* (1752) | `meier-para-N`, from `../meier-auszug.js` |

Each file is `{ "meta": …, "titles": …, "blocks": { "<block id>": "<English>" } }`.
`titles` holds the sections' titles and manuscript headings (`title:<section id>`,
`heading:<section id>:<n>`; for Meier `title:mNN` and `part:mNN`).

**These are unreviewed machine translations by Claude**, made block by block. Ruled
rule blocks have no entry. Latin terms are left in Latin, and the editorial brackets of
the Academy Edition (`[ Textlücke: AA: … ]`, `< bricht ab >`) are carried through
unchanged. Where a source block is only a sentence fragment, its translation is a
fragment too. A scholar should check them before they are quoted.

## Sentence-aligned Meier

`meier-aligned.json` pairs Meier's German with the English sentence by sentence
(`{ "para", "seg", "de", "en" }`, in § order). It is built by `../align-meier.py` from
`../meier-auszug.js` and `meier-en.json`. Where two or more sentences on one side answer
to one on the other, they are joined into one segment, which carries `"merged": {"de": n,
"en": m}`. Segments never cross a § boundary. The source runs the next section's heading
straight on after some §§ ("…Erkenntniss.von der Weitläuftigkeit…"); those headings are
kept as their own segments, as they are in the translation. Five segments are merged, all in
§§ 387–389 (a garbled syllogism table) and § 546.

`meier-aligned.js` is the same alignment as a classic script (`MEIER_ALIGNED`, keyed by §,
each row the sentence's character offsets into Meier's § text plus its English). It is
generated by `align-meier.py` along with the JSON and is what the page reads: in the Meier
tab every sentence is a unit: hovering it floats its English over the sentence, and clicking pins
it until Esc or a click elsewhere. The English is the only thing shown.

## Meier broken into phrases (not displayed)

`meier-phrases.json` is a copy of `meier-aligned.json` in which every sentence unit also
carries `"phrases": [{ "de", "en" }, …]`: the German cut into grammatical phrases, each with a
short English gloss. For example, *Bei der Wahrheit* — in the truth · *der unreinen Urtheile* —
of impure judgments · *muss man sonderlich,* — one must pay · *auf die Bestimmung* — to the
determination · *des Verbindungsbegriffs* — of the concept of connection · *und der Verneinung
desselben,* — and the negation of it · *Achtung geben.* — particular attention. The German
phrases concatenated reproduce the sentence exactly (checked for all 1697 units, ignoring
whitespace); punctuation stays on the phrase it follows. The glosses are unreviewed machine
output and may follow German word order. The page reads it through `meier-phrases.js` (see below).
The originals (`meier-aligned.json`, `meier-aligned.js`, `meier-en.json`) are unchanged.

`meier-phrases.js` is the same breakdown as a classic script (`MEIER_PHRASES[§][k]`, each phrase as
character offsets into Meier's § text plus its English gloss), generated by
`../build-meier-phrases-js.py`. `busolt-reader.html` uses it: in the Meier tab every sentence is cut
into its divisions, and hovering a division floats that division's English over it with the whole
sentence's English beneath; hovering elsewhere in the sentence shows the sentence alone. The glosses
are unreviewed machine output.
