Kant Lab Glossary

One English rendering for every Kantian term, with its Latin and the spellings the transcripts actually use. Every translation of the corpus is held to it.

← Kant’s Logic reader

How to read this. English is the rendering every translation must use. Other renderings appear only under the stated condition, and anything struck through is wrong here. Occurrences counts the term in Meier, the three Logik transcripts and the Reflexionen, matched despite period spellings. Prototype shows how often the earlier machine translations (made before this glossary existed) used the agreed rendering where the German has the term. Every entry is a proposal until a reader of Kant’s German confirms it. The German column gives the modern spelling, and beneath it how the texts actually print the word, most frequent spelling first, with its count (hover a spelling to see which texts use it). Period spellings are in bold; the modern spelling, where the texts use it, in green. Searching finds an entry by any of its printed spellings. Beneath each entry are examples: one sentence from each of Logik Busolt, Logik Blomberg and Meier’s Auszug that uses the term, with the term in bold and its English (the reader’s sentence translation) beside it. Click a citation to open the sentence in the reader. The examples are chosen by rule (glossary.py examples: the English must use the agreed rendering, then whole sentences of middling length are preferred), not by hand, and the translations are unreviewed machine output.
