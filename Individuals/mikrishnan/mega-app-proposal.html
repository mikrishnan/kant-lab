The Logic Portal

A research instrument for the seminar: put a question to the whole logic corpus the moment it comes up in class.

What it is for

We are a seminar of researchers trying to work out how Kant's logic works. Whatever passage we hit, in a lecture, in Jäsche or in a published text, the same few questions come up, and today each one takes a different tool or a round of Ctrl-F. The portal answers them in one place, from any passage or term. Every answer is a set of passages, and from any of them you can ask again. So you move from lecture to textbook to annotation and back without leaving the instrument, and a trail keeps your route.

The questions it answers

  1. How is this formulated across the corpus?Every version of a doctrine, term or sentence in every text, dated, in German and Latin and in the old spellings. Feng's lexicon of forms · Jin's parallel passages · Alex's sentence matching
  2. Where is this in Meier?The § of the Auszug a passage is on, and how we know: stated by Blomberg's own headings, or inferred with a measured confidence. Jesus's Meier–Kant concordance
  3. What are the associated Reflexionen?Kant's notes on that §, dated by Adickes, and the notes that share its wording, including the ones Jäsche drew on. the Meier Reader (Sophia & Maria) · Alex's Jäsche tracing
  4. What kinds does Kant distinguish?For the doctrines students have classified: the kinds, with passages from every layer under each. Nik (immediate inferences) · Sophia (mediate) · Aidan (judgments) · Feng (principles)

Every answer is signed. Each result says whose work produced it and whether it is stated or inferred. A Contributors page shows each person's part. On Monday the demo walks one question through everyone's work.

Built to take much more data

  • One catalogue of texts. Each text is registered once: what it is (textbook, notes, lecture, published), its date, its AA volume, and where its transcription came from. Every question reads the catalogue, so a new text shows up everywhere without changing the app.
  • Converters, not hand work. The lecture extractor already keeps a table of works, so the other logic lectures in AA XXIV (Herder, Philippi, Pölitz, Dohna-Wundlacken) should need little more than an entry each. Published texts get a converter that writes the same shape.
  • Ready for a better edition. Links are stored as AA citations plus the quoted words, as Nik's examples already are, not as positions in a file. When a clean digital edition arrives, its text can replace ours and every link survives.
