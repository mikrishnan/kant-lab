>>> A hypothesis is the holding-for-true of a cognition, where I infer from its sufficiency its true consequences, e.g. when I know that there is fire beneath the earth. The cognition, then, which flows from many true conse{80}quences is a hypothesis. When a ground has nothing but true consequences, then it is a true ground, but I do not yet cognize it a priori, but a posteriori, or through a hypothesis. A hypothesis is a presupposition. A ground that has nothing but true consequences ceases to be a true opinion; it becomes certain. A hypothesis can never be brought to apodictic certainty. E.g. the system of Copernicus is only a hypothesis. From given consequences we cannot infer determinate grounds with certainty, unless it can also be shown that the consequences flow from a single ground. —
