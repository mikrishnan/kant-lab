Mediate inferences in Kant’s logic lectures and Reflexionen

Examples of mediate inferences (Vernunftschlüsse) in the Blomberg, Busolt and Wiener logic transcripts and in Kant’s Reflexionen on Meier’s Auszug and Baumgarten’s Metaphysica, each in its paragraph or Reflexion with the inference highlighted.

What is attested, what is not. The paragraph and Reflexion texts, the Reflexion numbers (Adickes’ numbering), the Academy Edition page numbers and the manuscript page numbers are the transcriptions’, as data/logik-*-24.js, data/reflexionen-16-meier.js and data/reflexionen-17-18-baumgarten.js carry them; a grey tag such as AA 24:673 marks where a new page begins. For a Reflexion the § of Meier or Baumgarten comes in one of three ways, and the card says which: from the Academy Edition’s own locus note (shown verbatim), from the heading of the block it stands in, or, for the Baumgarten Reflexionen, from the handbook page it is written on. Where the edition names no § the card says so. Which stretch counts as a mediate inference, and the label on each, is the compiler’s reading, not the edition’s. The list is a curated selection from a read of the texts, not an exhaustive census. Short notes are the compiler’s, not Kant’s or his students’.

Immediate inferences (Verstandesschlüsse: conversion, contraposition, subalternation and the like) are left out.
