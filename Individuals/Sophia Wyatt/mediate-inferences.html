Mediate inferences in Kant’s logic lectures and Reflexionen

Examples of mediate inferences (Vernunftschlüsse) in the Blomberg, Wiener and Busolt logic transcripts and in Kant’s Reflexionen on Meier’s Auszug and Baumgarten’s Metaphysica, and the places where Kant says what a mediate inference is.

What is attested, what is not. The text of every paragraph and Reflexion, the Reflexion numbers (Adickes’ numbering), the Academy Edition page numbers and the manuscript page numbers are the transcriptions’, as data/logik-*-24.js, data/reflexionen-16-meier.js and data/reflexionen-17-18-baumgarten.js carry them; a grey tag such as AA 24:673 marks where a new page begins. Baumgarten’s text comes from data/metaphysica-17.js; the text of Meier’s Auszug is copied from the Meier reader. For a Reflexion the § of Meier or Baumgarten comes in one of three ways, and the card says which: from the Academy Edition’s own locus note (shown verbatim), from the heading of the block it stands in, or, for Baumgarten, from the handbook page it is written on. Where the edition names no §, the card says so. The dating pill is our plain-language reading of Adickes’ phases, and his own expression is shown beneath it; the lectures carry the date the edition gives the course.

Which stretch counts as a mediate inference, and the label on each, is the compiler’s reading, not the edition’s, and the same goes for which passages count as definitions or principles. The lists are curated selections from a read of the texts, not exhaustive. Short notes are the compiler’s, not Kant’s or his students’. Each card opens on a window of context chosen for that passage; “more context” steps outward to the paragraph and then to the neighbouring paragraphs or list items. Immediate inferences (Verstandesschlüsse: conversion, contraposition, subalternation and the like) are left out of the Examples.
