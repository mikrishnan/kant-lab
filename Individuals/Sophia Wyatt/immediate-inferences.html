Immediate inferences in Kant’s logic lectures

What the Blomberg, Wiener and Busolt logic transcripts say about immediate inferences (consequentiae immediatae, Verstandesschlüsse): first Kant’s examples, then everything else he says about them. Each is shown in the paragraph it stands in, with its lecture and Academy Edition page.

Highlighted text is the example; this tint marks a rule or remark. A paragraph that holds both appears in both parts.

What is attested, what is not. The paragraph text, the page numbers (Academy Edition, AA XXIV) and the manuscript page numbers are the transcriptions’, as data/logik-*-24.js carries them; a grey tag such as AA 24:671 marks where a new page begins. Spelling, punctuation and the transcriptions’ oddities are kept as printed. Which stretch counts as an example, which as a rule or remark, and the topic label on each, is the compiler’s reading, not the edition’s. Short notes are the compiler’s, not Kant’s or his students’.

The list is a curated selection from a read of the three transcripts and aims to be complete for immediate inference proper (equipollence, subalternation, opposition, conversion, contraposition); the inferences by induction and analogy that Busolt also calls Verstandesschlüsse are a different use of the word and are left out, as are the mediate inferences, which are in their own list.
