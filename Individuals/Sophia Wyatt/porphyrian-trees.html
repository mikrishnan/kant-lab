A Visual History of Scholastic Logic

The Tree of Porphyry

From the Isagoge of the 3rd century to the printed logic manuals of the 18th — a survey of how logicians across twelve centuries rendered the great hierarchy of Substance in diagram and text.

The Arbor Porphyriana, or Tree of Porphyry, is a hierarchical diagram derived from the Isagoge (c. 268 AD) of Porphyry of Tyre, Plotinus's pupil, written as an introduction to Aristotle's Categories. It maps the five predicables — genus, species, differentia, proprium, accidens — into a branching structure descending from the summum genus Substantia down through intermediate genera and differentiating properties to the infimae species and ultimately to individual things. Though Porphyry himself did not draw the diagram, commentators from Boethius onward gave it visual form, and it became one of the most reproduced illustrations in the history of logic.
