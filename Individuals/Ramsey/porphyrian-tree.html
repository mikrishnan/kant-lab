Arbor Porphyriana

The Tree of Predicables — after Porphyry of Tyre, Isagoge (c. 268 AD)

Subaltern genus (divides further) Infima species Non-descended species Differentia

Schema of Differentiation

I

corporeal  ↔  incorporeal

Whether substance has magnitude and occupies a determinate place. This is the cut that keeps God clear of every bodily predicate below it.

II

animate  ↔  inanimate

Whether a body possesses an inner principle of self-movement and growth.

II

uncreated  ↔  created parallel division, on the incorporeal genus

Whether an incorporeal substance exists of itself or derives its being from another. God is the uncreated incorporeal; a spirit — angel or separated intellect — is the created one.

III

sentient  ↔  insensible

Whether a living thing can perceive and respond to its surroundings.

IV

rational  ↔  irrational

Whether an animal possesses logos, the faculty of discursive reason.

V

mortal  ↔  immortal

Whether the rational animal is subject to natural dissolution. Porphyry put the gods in the immortal slot; since that slot is corporeal, it is filled here by the celestial being — the heavens held to be living, rational and everlasting bodies (Plato, Timaeus 40a–b).

Each genus divides by a differentia and its logical negation into exactly two species. Dashed nodes are non-descended species, terminal in this diagram though divisible in principle. Reading downward traces the succession of ever more specific predicates; reading upward from any terminal node reconstructs its full definition.
