Type words that occur in a judgment (German, any spelling). Each example found is labelled with its forms.
An example is a simple sentence — one subject and predicate, no subordinate clause (only a hypothetical or disjunctive may have two, for ground–consequence or division) — whose content carries no weight in the lecture — it is about men, bodies, souls, God, gold, triangles, Caius, not about cognition, reason or truth — so that it shows a form rather than making a point. What its form rests on is then one of three things: named, the text calls it that form; premise / conclusion, it stands in a syllogism that goes through, which fixes its quantity and quality; or speculated, nothing says what it is and the form is read off the wording alone. A label underlined is named in the text, one with a green foot is required by the inference, a dashed one is speculated and can be wrong. Click any sentence on the left to read it the same way. The German is the transcription's, the English is machine translation.
