How the two modes are modelled

Wolffian. Concepts form a strict hierarchy built by logical division. Each species has one proximate genus; its content is the genus's content plus its differentia. Coordinate species under one division exclude each other. Containment in (content) and containment under (extension) are read off the same tree and are reciprocal. A predicate is analytic of a subject when it is a higher concept on the subject's line. Because division supplies opposition, "No S is P" is analytic when S and P sit on coordinate branches.

Leibnizian. Concepts are combinations of marks, coded in the manner of Leibniz's characteristic numbers of 1679: each distinct mark gets a prime, a concept's number is the product of its marks' primes, and P is contained in S when N(P) divides N(S). The same mark recurring on several branches is one mark, so classification can cross the tree, and any two concepts can be combined. The drawing puts every differentia in the top row as a singleton set and places each concept in the row for its number of marks; a line joins two sets when one includes the other with no displayed set between them. Concepts with the same mark set appear as one node. The calculus has no built-in exclusion between marks, so it treats every combination as a possible concept.

This is a reconstruction for teaching and exploration. Check it against Anderson's Part I before relying on the details.

Division types. Two members: dichotomy; three: trichotomy; four or more: polytomy. A dichotomy by contradictory marks (A / non-A) is exhaustive on logical grounds alone. For other divisions exhaustiveness rests on the basis of division (Kant, Jäsche Logik §113, ties polytomy to empirical cognition).
