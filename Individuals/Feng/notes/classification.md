# How Kant's logical principles are classified

*Proposed with Claude, adopted by the owner of this folder on 24 October 2026. This file is
the source of the grouping: [principles-lexicon.js](../principles-lexicon.js) and the search
page [principle-search.html](../principle-search.html) follow it. When a decision here
changes, the lexicon changes with it.*

## The criterion

One question sorts every principle: **what does it govern?**

1. **Judging**: whether a single judgement is true in its form. Kant's three formal
   criteria of truth: the principle of contradiction (with identity), of sufficient
   reason, and of excluded middle. Jäsche *Logik*, AA 9:52–53; already in Kant's hand in
   Refl. 2178, AA 16:260.
2. **Inferring**: whether one judgement follows from another. One principle for each kind
   of syllogism: categorical, hypothetical, disjunctive. Jäsche *Logik*, AA 9:122–130,
   where the division of syllogisms rests on the three relations (AA 9:122); Refl. 3218,
   AA 16:717.
3. **Things**: what holds of objects. Principles of Baumgarten's ontology in the
   *Metaphysica*, outside logic.

## Why this way

- **One question for all.** The earlier groups A–C mixed three criteria: A by source
  (Baumgarten §§ 7–24), B by topic, C by function. Now every principle is sorted by what it
  governs, and stands in exactly one group.
- **The divisions are Kant's.** Groups 1 and 2 are divisions Kant makes himself, in his
  notes and in the Jäsche *Logik*. The classification follows them instead of inventing its
  own.
- **It shows where logic ends.** The principles of group 3 are about things, not about the
  form of thought, and they occur almost only in Baumgarten and in Kant's notes on him (see
  the chart). Group 3 marks the boundary between logic and metaphysics.
- **The same principles work twice.** Sufficient reason is also the principle of the
  hypothetical syllogism ("Das Princip der hypothetischen Schlüsse ist der Satz des
  Grundes", AA 9:129), and excluded middle that of the disjunctive ("Das Princip der
  disjunctiven Schlüsse ist der Grundsatz des ausschließenden Dritten", AA 9:130). The
  principles of judging return as principles of inferring.

## Decisions on particular principles

- **Contradiction and identity** are one criterion in Jäsche ("der Satz des Widerspruchs
  und der Identität", AA 9:52). They are kept as two principles here, as Baumgarten (Met.
  §§ 7, 11) and Kant's notes treat them.
- **The "principle of determination"** ("unter zwey entgegengesetzten Urtheilen ist eines
  wahr", Refl. 3063) is excluded middle applied to judgements, and joins it in group 1. Kant
  distinguishes it from the principle of thoroughgoing determination (Refl. 5154), which is
  of things and stands in group 3.
- **The *dictum de omni et nullo*** joins *nota notae* as the principle of the categorical
  syllogism. Jäsche derives the dictum from *nota notae*, and says it is not the highest
  principle (AA 9:123).
- ***Posita ratione ponitur rationatum*** (Met. § 30) reads like modus ponens, but is
  Baumgarten's ontology of grounds, and stands in group 3. So do the principle of the
  consequent (Met. § 23) and of things connected both ways (Met. § 24).
- **The modal rules** (*ab esse ad posse valet consequentia*, Met. § 57) are a doubtful
  case: rules of inference, but found only in Baumgarten's ontology and Kant's notes on it.
  Provisionally in group 3.

## Passages checked and left out

- **Refl. 5202**: *Princip der Bestimmung* means sufficient reason there ("Principium
  rationis ist das Princip der Bestimmung der Dinge in der Zeitfolge"), not excluded middle.
- **Jäsche AA 9:130**: *modum ponentem* and *modum tollentem* are used there of the
  disjunctive syllogism ("ponendo tollentem", "tollendo ponentem"), and are not counted for
  the hypothetical. The dilemma on the same page (*a remotione consequentis ad negationem
  antecedentis*) is a hypothetical rule, and is counted.
- **Refl. 3752**: *posita ratione ponitur aliud* says only that ground and consequence are
  different, and is not the rule.

## Open questions

- **Is the arrangement Kant's or Jäsche's?** It is fully explicit only in the Jäsche
  *Logik* (1800), which Jäsche compiled. Refl. 2178 names the same three "logische (g
  formale) Arten die Warheit zu finden", but there the principle of contradiction alone
  yields possibility, impossibility and, indirectly, necessity. The Wiener Logik (AA 24:826)
  names only two, contradiction and sufficient reason. When does the arrangement take shape?
- **Why is sufficient reason the principle of assertoric judgement**, and excluded middle
  of apodictic (AA 9:53)? Each needs an argument.
- **Quantity and quality.** The table of judgements has four headings, and only relation
  and modality appear above. Blomberg pairs identity with affirmative and contradiction with
  negative judgements (AA 24:88), and Busolt does the same with *nota notae* and *repugnans
  notae* (AA 24:673). Do quality, and perhaps quantity, have principles of their own?
