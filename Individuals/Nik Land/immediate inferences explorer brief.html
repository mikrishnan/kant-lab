Proposed diagram panel

Clicking an example opens a panel on the right with one diagram that answers one question: what this inference does.

Example kindDiagramWhy this one
Conversion, contraposition, equipollenceSpheres (Euler circles), premise and conclusion side by side on the same drawingThe lectures explain these by the spheres of concepts (Blomberg AA 24:275, Busolt AA 24:671, Refl. 3189). Keeping the drawing identical and changing only the emphasis shows the Wiener point: the matter is the same, only the form differs.
Contradictory, contrary, subcontrary, subalternationSquare of opposition, with the passage's own judgements at the corners and the relation highlightedThis is the traditional figure. Kant's Refl. 3177 sets the judgements out the same way: universals above, particulars below.
Counter-examples ("also ist auch Caius sterblich")Nested spheres with the middle term markedIt shows why these are not immediate, as Refl. 3166 puts it: "kein Mittelbegrif".
Definition passages; rules without an instanceA map of the kinds, following Kant's own sorting in Refl. 3170, or a schematic S / P version of the kind's diagramEvery card gets a diagram, and none is invented from text the passage lacks.

Design rules:

  • Honest about provenance. Text from the passage is drawn solid; anything supplied (concept names, a missing premise) is dashed and labelled. The panel says the Academy Edition prints no figure.
  • Doesn't overclaim. Where the drawing shows more than the premise says, it is flagged. One case is Wiener's contraposition to a particular ("einiges Sterbliche ist nicht Mensch"). Another is Kant's rejected conversion in Refl. 3187, which picks out an empty region.
  • Easy to read. Labels go on the shapes rather than in a legend, and the geometry repeats across panels so they can be compared. The app's palette is kept, and meaning is carried by pattern and label as well as colour.
  • Usable. The panel is a third column on wide screens and slides over the list on narrow ones. Esc closes it and the keyboard works throughout. Each diagram carries a text description for screen readers.

Cost: about 600 lines in the page, plus a diagram field on 49 examples recording the concept names and how each judgement reads as subject and predicate. That field is the annotator's reading, marked as such. The work would be checked in headless Chrome, since the app's Browser preview can't start a server here.

Risk to know about

Data In the shared transcript data, a block whose first page break falls partway through records the page it ends on as its starting page. For example, busolt-s26-044 says AA 24:671, but its first 328 characters are on 670. My explorer works around this. inference-explorer.html, committed alongside it, does not, so it can cite a page one too high. The fix belongs in scripts/parse_logik.py, which Sophia Wyatt and Maria own.
