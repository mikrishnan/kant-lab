Immediate Inferences

Kant's examples of the consequentiae immediatae — equipollence, subalternation, opposition, conversion, contraposition — from the Blomberg, Wiener and Busolt logic transcripts (Academy Edition vol. 24) and from his own Reflexionen on Meier's Auszug (vol. 16). The examples are in the middle. On the left are the judgements they are made of, each with the transcript or Reflexion, the volume and the page. How to read this list

A E I O — universal affirmative, universal negative, particular affirmative, particular negative; S singular. A box with a solid edge: the passage itself says what form the judgement has. A dashed edge: the form is read off the quantifier and the copula here, and the passage does not state it.

Read as — the judgement written out where the passage leaves part of it to be understood (“einige”). It is not in the text.

Pages are the Academy pages (AA 24 for the transcripts, AA 16 for the Reflexionen). A transcript’s page is the page on which the sentence stands. A Reflexion’s is the whole range of the Reflexion, since the data does not say where inside it a sentence falls. A dashed underline on a § means it was taken from the Academy’s block heading, not the Reflexion’s own locus note.
