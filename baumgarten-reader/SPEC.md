# Baumgarten reader

*The spec is the source of truth for this tool. Claude builds what it says; reviewers
check the tool against it. When the tool's behaviour changes, this file changes in the
same pull request.*

*Drafted on 10 October 2026 from what the page did on that day, for its owners to review.
Every Behaviour item was checked by driving the page in headless Chrome, not by clicking
through it by hand. Items the page does not yet satisfy are marked **Fails at present**,
with the Open question that explains why. A pull request that makes one pass removes the
mark.*

## Question

Baumgarten's *Metaphysica* (4th edition, Halle 1757) is the textbook Kant lectured from
for forty years, and his interleaved copy of it carries most of the notes printed as the
Reflexionen zur Metaphysik. The tool lets a reader work through the Latin text § by § with
Baumgarten's own German equivalents at the words they translate. Beside it, Kant's notes
on the § in view are shown with Adickes' dating, the Academy Edition's statement of
where on the page each one stands, and, where the AA names them, the words Kant was
annotating. With the printed volumes, that means holding AA XVII open at the text, AA
XVII–XVIII at the Reflexionen and AA XIV at Adickes' table, and cross-referencing by hand.

## Sources

- **The text.** A. G. Baumgarten, *Metaphysica*, as AA XVII reprints it: the 804 §§ the
  volume prints (§§ 1–503 and 700–1000), AA 17:24–206, with the 1757 pagination it
  gives in brackets. From [../data/metaphysica-17.js](../data/metaphysica-17.js): the
  `MET_SECTIONS` outline, `MET_PARAGRAPHS`, `MET_BY_NUM`, and `MET_SYNOPSIS`, the
  Synopsis at AA 17:19–23. Generated from `Textfiles/Baumgarten.rtfd` by
  `scripts/parse_baum.py`.
- **Kant's notes.** 2967 Reflexionen, Refl. 3489–6455: the Erläuterungen printed
  beneath the text in AA XVII and the Reflexionen zur Metaphysik at AA 17:227–745 and
  AA 18:5–725. From
  [../data/reflexionen-17-18-baumgarten.js](../data/reflexionen-17-18-baumgarten.js):
  `REFLM_META`, `REFLM_ENTRIES` and `REFLM_BY_PARA`. Generated by `scripts/parse_refl.py`
  and `scripts/emit_baum_refl.py`.
- **The dating.** Adickes' chronology of the 33 phases of Kant's hand, AA 14:XXXV–XLIII,
  from [../data/phases-adickes.js](../data/phases-adickes.js): `phaseInfo()` and
  `ADICKES_PREAMBLE`. Written by hand from `Textfiles/vol14Adickes.rtf`.
- **One hand-entered table** in the page itself, `KANT_REFS`: the phases and pages of
  the "Handschriftliche Bemerkungen Kants" block the volume prints at the Prolegomena
  and at Sectio I · Possibile, and nowhere else.

The commands that regenerate the two generated files are in "Regenerating the generated
data" in the root [CLAUDE.md](../CLAUDE.md).

## Attested vs. inferred

**Shown as the Academy Edition prints it.** Baumgarten's Latin, spelling and suspected
misprints included; the marker the volume prints at each German equivalent, even where
its numbering restarts or is wrong; the AA and 1757 page numbers; the AA's note standing
where §§ 504–699 would be; the Synopsis's wording and markers. For each Reflexion: the
text, Adickes' phase expression verbatim on an `AA:` line whenever it says more than the
phase chips do (query marks, parentheses, ranges, prose datings such as `70er Jahre`),
the locus note verbatim, and Kant's page siglum.

**Worked out by the tool, and marked as such:**

- **Which § a Reflexion belongs to.** Every card carries a tag saying how its § was
  arrived at, with a tooltip explaining it:

  | Tag | Meaning | Entries |
  | --- | --- | --- |
  | `§ attested` | the § is named in the note's own locus note | 1648 |
  | `§ from handbook page` | the AA gives only a page of Kant's copy; these are all the §§ the 1757 edition prints on that page | 31 |
  | `§ from AA block` | the § range comes from the AA block heading the note stands under | 557 |
  | `interpolated §` | the AA states no §; the tool inferred one from the neighbouring entries. The card also has a dashed left border | 23 |
  | `front matter`, `loses Blatt`, `another handbook`, `no § stated` | the note belongs to no § and is filed under none | 459, 91, 107, 51 |

- **A suspected misprint** is underlined, never corrected. The conjecture is printed
  beneath the § as "so AA XVII; perhaps a misprint for …".
- **A phase chip's years and colour** come from Adickes' table. The colour groups phases
  by the decade their dating begins in. That grouping is the tool's own; the years are
  Adickes'. A dashed chip marked `undat.` means he gives no date.
- **Sentence numbers** for a `Satz` chip are counted by splitting on sentence-final
  punctuation, and the chip's tooltip says the count is approximate.
- **Finding a lemma** in the Latin uses an exact match, then a case-insensitive one, and
  nothing looser. Where neither finds the words, nothing is highlighted.
- **The table of contents' labels** are each §'s first six words, not titles Baumgarten
  gave. The headings above them are his.
- **Gaps are stated, not papered over.** The Synopsis says where the transcription lacks
  pages, and does not link the §§ AA XVII does not print.

Two places where the display does **not** yet meet this rule are Open questions 2 and 3.

## Behaviour

*Items are never renumbered. When one is dropped, strike it through and say why —
`~~3. …~~ (dropped: superseded by 7)` — so that a pull request saying "implements item 4"
keeps its meaning.*

### Opening the page

1. The page opens from the site and straight from disk (double-clicking the file), and
   the browser console shows no errors.
2. The reading column shows all 804 §§ that AA XVII prints, §§ 1–503 and 700–1000, under
   Baumgarten's part, chapter and section headings, with his Roman numerals as printed
   (`Pars IIII · Theologia Naturalis`, not IV).
3. Under `Caput I · Psychologia Empirica`, where §§ 504–699 would stand, the AA's own
   note reads "Sectio I-XVIII (§. 504-699) sind in Bd. XV S. 5-54 abgedruckt." It appears
   in the reading column and in the table of contents.

### The text

4. Each § opens with its number, a green `AA 17:` tag giving the page or pages it stands
   on, and a grey `1757 p.` tag giving Baumgarten's own page. § 4 shows `AA 17:24` and
   `1757 p. 2`. §§ 1–3 have no AA tag, because they come before the volume's first page
   marker.
5. Baumgarten's German equivalents appear as amber chips at the point in the Latin where
   he gives them, each labelled with the marker the volume prints. At § 4, *Die
   Grund-Wissenschaft* follows *Ontologia*. There are 710 of them.
6. Every cross-reference in the text (`§. 14`) is shown as `§14` with a dotted underline,
   and clicking it goes to that §, as item 12 describes.
7. At § 10 the word *praepositio*, and at § 92 *methaphysice* and *methaphysica*, are
   printed as the volume has them, underlined with a dotted rule. A note beneath each §
   reads "… so AA XVII; perhaps a misprint for …" and gives the reason.
8. At the head of the Prolegomena and of `Sectio I · Possibile`, a purple block headed
   "Handschriftliche Bemerkungen Kants" lists the phases and AA pages the volume gives
   there. The Prolegomena's are `Phase δ · XVII · 249`, `Phase λ · XVII · 438` and
   `Phase ν · XVII · 527`.
9. Clicking anywhere in a § highlights it and marks its entry in the table of contents.

### Table of contents

10. The left sidebar lists every § under Baumgarten's headings, each entry giving the §
    number and its opening words. Clicking a part's heading collapses or expands it, and
    its arrow turns between ▾ and ▸.
11. Typing in `Filter §§ …` hides every entry whose number and opening words do not
    contain what was typed. `ratio` leaves 56 entries; `14` leaves § 14 together with
    §§ 114, 140–149 and so on. Emptying the box shows every entry again.
12. Clicking an entry scrolls the text until that § is in the middle of the screen,
    highlights it, and marks the entry.
13. `☰ TOC` in the top bar hides the sidebar, and pressing it again brings the sidebar
    back.

### The Synopsis

14. `Synopsis` in the top bar opens Baumgarten's conspectus (AA 17:19–23) over the page.
    A note at the top says that its printed indentation is not reproduced, and that the
    markers stand in a gutter exactly as the volume letters them. The 1757 page numbers
    (`1757, p. XLIV` …) stand where each page begins.
15. Every § reference in the Synopsis is a link. Clicking one closes the Synopsis and goes
    to the first § it names: `§. 7-18` goes to § 7. References to §§ AA XVII does not
    print, such as `§. 519-533`, are shown but not linked, and their tooltip says "AA XVII
    does not print this §".
16. Where the transcription lacks two pages of the Synopsis, a note reads "§§ 280–518 —
    the Synopsis covers these, but the pages carrying them are not in the source
    transcription."
17. `Close`, a click on the dimmed page behind, or Escape closes the Synopsis.

### The Reflexionen panel

18. On opening, the panel on the right shows § 1: the § number, `20 Reflexionen`, and a
    card for each. The panel's header shows the same count as `20 notes`, and its count
    always matches the cards shown.
19. Each card shows the Refl. number; a chip for each phase of Kant's hand, with Adickes'
    years and coloured by decade; the AA page; the AA's dating verbatim on an `AA:` line
    where it says more than the chips do; the AA's locus note verbatim; the text of the
    note; Kant's page siglum; and the tag saying how the § was arrived at. A glyph in the
    card's gutter shows where on the page the note stands (→ Zu, ‖ Gegenüber, ▎ Neben,
    ↕ Zwischen, ↑ Über, ↓ Unter, » Nach, ⊂ In, · none given), and its tooltip says what
    the AA's word means.
20. Cards whose § is named in their own locus note come first, then those placed by
    handbook page, then those placed by AA block, each run in order of Refl. number.
21. Interpolated cards have a dashed left border and a dashed `interpolated §` tag. § 926
    has six of them, Refl. 3929–3934.
22. A § with no Reflexion says so: at § 33, "The Academy Edition records no Reflexion at
    §33."
23. At the foot of the panel, `708 Reflexionen belong to no §` opens all 708 in AA order:
    459 tagged `front matter`, 91 `loses Blatt`, 107 `another handbook` and 51 `no §
    stated`. `‹ back to §N` returns to the § the panel was showing.
24. `×` in the panel's header hides the panel. A `Reflexionen` button then appears in the
    top bar, and pressing it brings the panel back.
25. Dragging the bar between the text and the panel resizes the panel, between about 260
    and 640 pixels wide.

### Following the text

26. The top bar, the table of contents and the panel stay on screen while the text
    scrolls; only the text column moves. **Fails at present:** see Open question 1.
27. As the text scrolls, the panel follows to the § at the top of the column.
    **Fails at present:** see Open question 1.
28. After any jump (a table-of-contents entry, a cross-reference, a Synopsis link, a click
    on a §), the panel shows the § jumped to, and still shows it once the scrolling has
    stopped. **Fails at present:** see Open question 1.
29. Bringing the panel back with `Reflexionen` shows the § the reader is on.
    **Fails at present:** it shows § 1. See Open question 1.

### Adickes' notes on the phases

30. Clicking a phase chip opens Adickes' note on that phase: its years, a separate block
    for every qualification he attaches to the dating, his note verbatim, and his remarks
    on the notation generally (AA 14:XXXV–XXXVI). On Refl. 3710 at § 1, the chip `δ`
    opens "Phase δ", dated "um 1762–63". The screen shows "vm 1762–63" until Open
    question 2 is settled.
31. `close`, a click on the dimmed page behind, or Escape closes it.

### Finding the words a note is on

32. Clicking a lemma chip (`»…«`), or anywhere else on a card that has one, tints those
    words in the Latin and scrolls to them. The panel is not rebuilt, so the card stays
    where it was. At § 11, Refl. 3489's `»quicquid est, illud«` tints *quicquid est,
    illud*.
33. Where the AA abbreviates a lemma to its first and last words, the tint runs from the
    first to the next occurrence of the last, and skips the German chips in between. At
    § 12, Refl. 3490's `»Posito — quod«` tints *Posito impossibili oritur contradictio*
    through *quod*.
34. Of the 82 lemmata, 75 are found. The other 7 tint nothing, because the AA is not
    quoting the printed words (Out of scope).
35. Clicking a `Satz` chip tints that sentence of the §. At § 15, Refl. 3510's `Satz 2`
    tints the sentence beginning *Quod nec in se quidem*. The chip's tooltip says that
    sentence counting is approximate.
36. Where the locus note says where in the § the note stands, the card shows it as a
    chip: `Anfang` on Refl. 3498 at § 14. If the note also has a lemma or a `Satz`,
    highlighting it puts a ⌐ mark at the start or the end of the § as well. Refl. 3525 at
    § 37 (`Schluss von Satz 1`) does this. 5 of the 88 such notes have a lemma or `Satz`;
    the other 83, Refl. 3498 among them, show the chip and no mark (Open question 7).
37. Each new highlight clears the previous one.

## Out of scope

- **§§ 504–699.** AA XVII does not reprint the *Psychologia empirica*; it is in AA XV.
  Supplying it from another source would need its provenance marked throughout.
- **Baumgarten's three prefaces.** The reader is keyed to § numbers, and they come
  before § 1.
- **Rebuilding the Synopsis's indentation.** It is in neither the RTF nor the text
  conversion, and the markers cannot supply it: the volume letters several levels in
  Greek, and the transcription renders some of those as Latin lookalikes.
- **Correcting the text.** Suspected misprints are flagged (item 7). Text errors are
  corrected in `scripts/parse_baum.py`, never in the page or by hand in `data/`.
- **Looser lemma matching.** The 7 lemmata that are not found must stay unfound. The AA
  normalises Baumgarten's spelling there (»subposita« for *supposita*), cites Kant's
  German, or composes a topic label of its own. Marking nearby words would put the mark
  where Kant did not write.
- **A second work.** The Meier reader has its own page. A Meier tab here, or a Baumgarten
  tab there, has been tried and removed.
- **One shared Reflexionen panel for both readers.** AA XVI and AA XVII–XVIII are built
  differently, and so are their panels.
- **Saving anything.** Nothing is stored between visits, and nothing is edited in the
  browser.

## Open questions

1. **The page scrolls as a whole, so the panel cannot follow the text** (items 26–29).
   This is the "scroll problem" reported from the live site on 3 October. It has two
   layers, and they have to be fixed together.
   - The page's `<body>` has `min-height: 100vh` where it needs a fixed `height`. So
     the text column grows to its full length, about 132,000 pixels, and the whole page
     scrolls instead of the column. After a jump to § 300, the top bar, the table of
     contents and the panel are 40,000 pixels above the window, leaving two empty
     columns. The panel's scroll-follower watches the column, which now never scrolls, so
     it does nothing on scrolling. It does fire whenever the layout changes, which is why
     reopening the panel shows § 1.
   - With the height fixed (tried in a test browser, not in the file), the panel follows
     scrolling. But it also follows the programmatic scroll of every jump. Clicking § 50
     in the table of contents leaves the panel on § 47, the § in the panel's tracking band
     when the target is centred. Clicking a lemma chip rebuilds the panel under the
     reader, so the card disappears, which breaks item 32. The scroll-follower has to
     stand aside while the tool's own jumps are scrolling.

   A fix should make items 26–29 pass and keep items 12 and 32 passing.
2. **The display turns u into v.** The page declares `<html lang="la">`, and for Latin
   the EB Garamond font substitutes v for u. The data reads *aut*, *quod*, *verum*; the
   page shows *avt*, *qvod*, *vervm*. It does the same to every German text on the page
   that is not in italics: Kant's notes in the panel (*Grvndsätze*), Adickes' note in
   the phase overlay (*Bavmgartens*, *vnd*), and Adickes' years on the phase chips
   (`vm 1762–63` for *um*). For the German this is plainly wrong. For the Latin, it shows
   letters the transcription does not have. Is the classical v wanted for the Latin at
   all? If not, setting `lang="la"` on the Latin text only, and `lang="de"` on the
   German, would end it. Either way, the hard rule says the reader must be able to see
   what the edition prints.
3. **Ten notes say `no § stated` while their own locus note names a §**: Refl. 3531
   (`ZuM §. 58 Schluss`), 3629, 3630, 3646, 3694, 3709, 3806, 4058, 4266 and 4660. All
   ten are filed under no §. They are part of the 51 that the docs count as having no §.
   Eight of them show a second symptom: the end of the entry's header (part of the phase
   expression and the siglum, such as `ψ−χ?? M 16.`) has run into the text of the note,
   and the phase expression is cut short (`δ?η2? (κ3?`). About 40 entries in all have
   this header debris: 4 in the Erläuterungen, 12 in AA XVII and 24 in AA XVIII. The fix
   belongs in `scripts/parse_refl.py` or `scripts/emit_baum_refl.py`. After it, the ten
   should be filed under their §s and leave the 51.
4. **Should the table of contents follow the scrolling too?** Its highlight moves only on
   a jump or a click, never on scrolling. The handoff notes assumed it followed. Once
   Open question 1 is settled, decide whether it should.
5. **Cross-references into the §§ 504–699 gap.** In the text they are links that go
   nowhere: § 700 opens with `Mutor, §. 505-699`. The Synopsis shows the same references
   unlinked, with a tooltip (item 15). Should the text do the same?
6. **Only the first number of a run is linked.** In `§. 20, 23`, 20 is a link and 23 is
   plain text. The Meier reader links a run to its first § too, but names the rest in the
   tooltip. Should either reader link every number?
7. **Should a card that says only `Anfang` or `Schluss` be clickable too?** It shows the
   chip (item 36), but clicking it does nothing, since it has no lemma or `Satz` to
   highlight. The ⌐ mark could still show where in the § the note stands.
